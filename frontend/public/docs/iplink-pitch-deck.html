The problem

The income is real.
Proving it isn't.

A musician earning $2,000 a month from her catalog has genuinely earned it. But a bank can't audit Spotify's payout ledger, and an on-chain lender can't read a distributor's database. So the income sits there — recurring, real, and unusable as collateral.

Why it stays unsolved

Every workaround puts someone back in the middle.

Verification has fixed costs. Those costs don't scale down — which is why underwriting has a floor, and why everything below it goes unfunded.

Approach one

Platform dashboards

A screenshot of earnings is a claim, not evidence. It can be edited, staged, or simply wrong — and no lender will underwrite against it.

Approach two

API integrations

Better, but you're trusting that the endpoint is genuine and unspoofed. Each new platform is another bespoke integration to build and maintain.

Approach three

Oracle operators

An operator asserting "this payment happened" is precisely the single point of trust that on-chain finance exists to remove.

Market

The capital exists.
It just has a floor.

Institutional money has already decided royalty income is a real asset class. Publishers pay 18–26× annual revenue for verified catalogs. Blackstone committed over $1.2B between 2021 and 2025.

But financing platforms want roughly $25,000 a year in royalties before they'll look at you. Below that line, verifying the income costs more than the deal is worth.

That floor is a verification problem, not a risk problem.

Why Creditcoin

The thesis already matched.

Creditcoin exists to make credit history verifiable on-chain — so that a proven track record can do the job collateral usually does. It was built for borrowers no institution could underwrite.

That is exactly the creator's problem, in a different asset class. We didn't choose Creditcoin because it was available. We chose it because the problem was the same one.

The attested credit record

Every verified payment becomes a permanent, unfakeable entry in that creator's history. Aggregated across campaigns, it forms a portable track record an investor can check before funding the next raise.

Structurally the same primitive as loan-repayment history — applied to royalties.

07
Why Attestcoin

Not a bridge. Not an oracle.

Three approaches could connect off-chain revenue to on-chain financing. Only one avoids reintroducing a middleman.

Rejected

A token bridge

Bridges move assets between chains. IPlink doesn't need to move anything — it needs to prove a fact occurred. Right tool, wrong problem.

Rejected

A centralized oracle

An operator reporting "this payment happened" reintroduces the exact single point of trust that verification is meant to remove.

Chosen

Attestcoin Protocol

Proves transaction inclusion using Merkle and continuity proofs, verified natively on Creditcoin in roughly one block. No operator, no async wait, no trust assumption.

Security

We audited ourselves and published the results.

Ten issues found, several severe. All fixed in the deployed contracts, each with a test in the repository.

Share token transfer drain — shares made non-transferable
Capital release over-withdrawal — fixed amount, once only
Cross-campaign proof replay — proofs bound to one campaign
Double-investment erasing earnings — settle before rebalance
Missing refunds, false completion, bricking, self-investment, dust

Plus a 40% reserve held in every campaign so investor payouts don't depend on a creator depositing funds later.

Creator abandonment is bounded, not solved

A creator can take their 60% and never verify revenue. Investors recover at most the reserve. No contract can compel someone to earn money.

Verification proves payment, not legitimacy

A proof confirms a transaction happened — not that it came from genuine royalties. Production requires registering a distributor's real payout contract at onboarding.
