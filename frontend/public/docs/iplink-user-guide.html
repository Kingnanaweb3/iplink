User guide

How to use IPlink, whether you're raising capital against your revenue or investing in someone who is.

Before you start

IPlink runs on public testnets. Tokens have no real value, and nothing here involves real money.

You'll need:

  • A browser wallet — MetaMask, Rabby, or similar
  • Creditcoin testnet CTC for gas and investing (from a testnet faucet)
  • Sepolia ETH if you plan to trigger a revenue payment

Open the app and click Connect wallet. IPlink will prompt you to switch networks when an action needs a different chain.

Finding a campaign

The campaigns page lists every live raise. Each card shows the asset, how much has been raised, and the revenue share on offer.

Status labels mean:

  • Raising — still open for investment
  • Funded — goal met, no longer accepting investment
  • Revenue verified — at least one payment has been proven on-chain
  • Completed — the return cap was reached and rights reverted to the creator

Checking a creator before you invest

On any campaign, click View attested credit record. This shows everything that creator has verified across all their campaigns — total raised, number of verified revenue events, and a timeline of each one with its block number.

No creator can add an entry to this record themselves. Each row only exists because a real payment was cryptographically proven on-chain. An empty record isn't necessarily bad — it may just be a new creator — but it does mean there's no track record to underwrite yet.

Investing in a campaign

1

Read the terms

Check the revenue share percentage, the return cap (the maximum you'll be repaid in total), and when the term ends.

2

Enter an amount

The page shows how much is left to fully fund the campaign. Use Max to fill exactly that. You can't invest more than the remaining amount.

3

Confirm in your wallet

Your share tokens are minted immediately. They represent your proportional claim on verified revenue.

Share tokens can't be transferred or sold. This is deliberate — the payout accounting can't safely support transfers yet. A secondary market is on the roadmap, not in this version.

Claiming your payouts

When revenue is verified, your share is calculated automatically and a payout panel appears on the campaign page showing what you're owed.

Click Claim payout and confirm. Funds go straight to your wallet.

There's no deadline — unclaimed payouts accumulate, so you can claim once after several revenue events rather than each time.

Getting a refund

If a campaign doesn't reach its goal before its funding window closes, you can withdraw your full investment.

Return to the campaign page after the funding deadline and click Withdraw investment. Your share tokens are burned and your contribution is returned in full.

Raising capital as a creator

A campaign is defined by four things:

  • Raise amount — how much capital you want
  • Revenue share — the percentage of verified revenue that goes to investors
  • Return cap — the total investors are repaid before rights revert to you
  • Term length — when the arrangement ends regardless of repayment

You keep ownership of your IP throughout. You're selling a share of revenue for a defined period, not the asset.

Campaign creation currently runs through a deployment script rather than the interface. A creation UI is on the roadmap.

Receiving your funds

Once the goal is met, Release capital appears under Creator actions.

You receive 60% of the raise. The remaining 40% stays in the contract as a reserve to cover investor payouts, so investors aren't dependent on you depositing funds later.

If verified revenue eventually exceeds what the reserve covers, use Deposit payout funds to top it up.

Verifying revenue

Revenue only counts once it's been proven on-chain. The process runs in three steps, visible on the campaign page:

1

Payment lands on the source chain

A payment is made through the registered payer contract. In this build that's a testnet stand-in for a real distributor.

2

Inclusion proof is generated

The Attestcoin Protocol produces cryptographic evidence that the payment was included in a real block. This takes a few minutes while the block is attested.

3

Verified on Creditcoin

The proof is checked on-chain. Only then does the campaign credit investors.

Anyone can submit a proof — creator, investor, or a complete stranger. No permission is required, because the proof itself is what's trusted, not whoever submits it.

Risks worth understanding

This is unsecured financing

There's no collateral. If a creator stops earning, or takes their capital and never verifies any revenue, investors recover at most the 40% reserve. No smart contract can force someone to earn money.

Verification proves payment, not legitimacy

A proof confirms a transaction genuinely happened. It can't confirm the money came from real royalties rather than the creator paying themselves. In production this is handled by registering a distributor's actual payout contract during onboarding.

Share tokens are illiquid

You can't sell your position. Your capital is committed until the return cap is reached or the term ends.

Common questions

Is this a loan?

No. There's no interest rate and no repayment schedule. Investors are repaid only from revenue that arrives, capped at an agreed multiple.

Why does the payment happen on a different chain?

Because that's the point. Real royalty income doesn't originate on Creditcoin — it comes from the outside world. IPlink's value is proving that outside payment happened, without trusting anyone's word for it.

Why does verification take a few minutes?

The source-chain block has to be attested on Creditcoin before a proof can be generated. That's a periodic cycle, not instant. Once attested, verification itself takes about one block.

What happens when the return cap is reached?

The campaign closes and all future revenue belongs entirely to the creator again.

Can I invest in my own campaign?

No — the contract rejects it, to prevent creators faking traction or routing their own payouts.
